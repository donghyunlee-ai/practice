A distribution report with density curves and raw data points

Write code · Challenge · §8.5 Choosing a chart and reporting a distribution

Make a one-page distribution report on how many hours a week students worked part-time, for students who live in the dorm (dorm) and students who commute from home (home). Make Axes in 2 rows and 1 column so that the two Axes share the x-axis, and put the unit and the numbers of observations in the figure title, like Weekly part-time hours (Dorm 10, Home 8).

On the top Axes, overlay the density curves of the two groups with seaborn's kdeplot() and cut=0. Draw dorm first as a solid line and home next as a dashed line (linestyle="--"), and show a legend with the labels Dorm and Home. The title is Density curves.

On the bottom Axes, plot one dot per student. Put the part-time hours on the x-axis and the group names (Dorm, Home) on the y-axis, and plot dorm first. To keep the dots from being jittered (shifted slightly at random), pass jitter=False to seaborn's stripplot(), or plot them with Matplotlib's scatter(). The title is One dot per student, and only the top Axes has a legend.

The x-axis label that states the unit (Hours per week) is not graded. Grading redraws the charts with different values in dorm and home.
